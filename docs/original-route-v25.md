# Original Route v2.5 — Boss identity rebuild

## Boundary

The original route does not define the card's story. UBR receives a fixed battle ID and player profile, then presents the enemy arrival, combat rules, phase changes, and battle result. No archive/training/trial/lesson narrative is generated by the runtime.

The player may use the Ultraman powers already made available by the story. UBR does not re-lock them behind an ADAPT meter.

## 1. Zetton → Hyper Zetton

### Zetton

The first phase changes defense from free movement to a four-direction guard. This borrows the idea of a boss changing the player's defensive grammar, but every threat is expressed through Zetton's own combat language:

- one-trillion-degree fireball pressure;
- wave-like directional returns;
- teleport feints;
- barrier stance;
- high-output energy attacks can be absorbed and stored for a later return volley.

The player remains at the center and turns the defense toward the incoming side.

### Hyper Zetton Imago

At the phase shift, the DOM model changes to the slimmer Imago silhouette and the battle returns to free movement. Hyper Zetton uses:

- teleport rushes and afterimages;
- dark fireball volleys;
- diagonal/high-speed returns;
- stronger beam absorption and amplified return fire.

The phase change therefore changes both the enemy body and the player's defensive rules.

## 2. Greeza — three forms

### First form

Greeza appears as a distorted sphere. Direct player attacks do not reduce HP: the body slips away and the combat UI shows `MISS`. After several failed contacts, the sphere collapses into the second form inside the normal combat scene.

### Second form

The visible body is not a reliable indication of attack origin. Displacement rushes, rear beams and warped projectiles deliberately disagree with the sprite's apparent position. At a predictable cadence, a light normal attack can still miss as the body slips away.

### Final form

The final form becomes fully tangible. In exchange, its model gains additional absorbed-organ shapes and its attack vocabulary expands to monster-derived fireballs, lightning, shock rings, impact rushes and multi-angle beams.

The three-form arc is therefore: cannot hit → can partly track → can fully hit, but must survive the widest arsenal.

## 3. Grand King — advancing heavy fortress

There is no vent/seam minigame and no visible ARMOR objective.

- Grand King's body creates a persistent front line that physically advances into the battle box.
- The front pushes the player downward instead of dealing arbitrary wall damage.
- Head/body cannons overlap vertical lanes, horizontal lanes, sweeps, shock rings and body rushes.
- Early armor reduces incoming damage; later HP phases raise penetration as the model visibly heats and cracks.
- Later phases add more direct body pressure while the safe arena is smaller.

The monster itself takes space away from the player.

## 4. Five King — direct body destruction

Five King owns five real modules whose HP totals the encounter HP:

- Fire Golza head;
- Melba wings;
- Gan-Q left arm;
- Reicubas right arm;
- Super C.O.V. chest/core.

The ACT menu can select a live body part without consuming an enemy turn. Player damage is applied to that selected module.

When a module reaches zero:

- the matching DOM body component dims, shrinks or goes dark;
- its attack family is removed from all future enemy turns;
- the next surviving body part becomes the default target.

Enemy turns only draw attacks from surviving organs. With three or fewer parts alive, two remaining organs can combine their patterns.

## 5. Ultraman Belial — weapon duel

Belial is rendered as a complete dark Ultraman silhouette with legs, torso, eyes, timer and a full-length Giga Battlenizer.

### Duel phase

The weapon enters the battle box as an actual sweeping hazard:

- blue Battlenizer sweep: moving is unsafe; stop to let it pass;
- orange Battlenizer sweep: standing still is unsafe; keep moving;
- thrusts, Deathscythe-style lines and lightning overlap the staff rules.

### Monster-army phase

Monster silhouettes rush from the sides while Belial continues using the Battlenizer. Belial does not step out of the fight and become a passive emitter.

### Last duel / beam clash

The army withdraws and the fight returns to Belial. When normal HP reaches zero, victory is intercepted. The Battlenizer drops inside the normal battle frame, Belial charges a dark-red beam, and the player must push the clash back with repeated confirm input before true victory is emitted.

## Model presentation

The five original bosses no longer rely on one generic `head/body/arms` silhouette with color changes alone. Each enemy has a specific DOM composition and in-combat pose classes. Phase changes stay in the combat scene when possible; fullscreen story cinematics are not used as a substitute for the enemy model acting during battle.
